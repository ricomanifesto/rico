A lot of the tools on this site started the same way: I wanted a calmer way to keep up with security news without spending my mornings scrolling feeds and trying to remember which advisory actually mattered.

Over the last few months, [SentryInsight](https://sentryinsight.ricomanifesto.com/) and [GRCInsight](https://grcinsight.ricomanifesto.com/) have been collecting the raw pieces—actively exploited vulnerabilities, notable threat activity, and the governance or regulatory shifts that end up landing on security teams anyway. For a while I was just rolling those notes up into a short digest for myself.

I’ve decided to share it for free as a small email newsletter called **Security & GRC Notes**.

It isn’t a breaking-news wire and it isn’t a vendor roundup. Each issue covers a handful of security and GRC developments that seem worth paying attention to, with a short summary, what is still open or uncertain, and direct links to the primary sources behind each item. If a claim rests only on a short excerpt or an early report, the issue says so plainly. At launch it goes out by email only, without a public web archive.

Here is how it is put together: automated gathering pulls candidate items from monitored feeds and organizes the source links, and then I review, edit, verify against the primary sources, and approve every issue by hand before anything is sent. Nothing goes out on a timer without that review pass first.

The cadence is roughly weekly. If it is a quiet week—or if I haven’t had time to review an issue properly—I’ll skip a week rather than pad an email with filler.

There is no account to create. Only your email address, whether you are subscribed, and basic delivery status like a bounce are stored. I don’t use open-tracking pixels or click tracking, and I don’t share or sell the list. You confirm your address once before receiving anything, and every email lets you leave in one click.

If that sounds useful, you can subscribe at [newsletter.ricomanifesto.com](https://newsletter.ricomanifesto.com/).

If you read an issue and spot something I missed or got wrong, just reply to it; replies come straight to me. Keeping this small and hand-reviewed is the whole point, so it stays grounded in primary evidence and useful to people doing the work.
